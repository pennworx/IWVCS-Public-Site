Common situations

Answers for hunters, anglers, and trappers.

Does a suspension in one state mean I am suspended everywhere?

It can affect privileges in every participating state, but the result is not automatically identical everywhere. A receiving state generally recognizes the suspension when the underlying violation would also support suspension under its law. Contact each state where you intend to hunt, fish, trap, or obtain a license.

How do I find out whether I am eligible in another state?

Ask that state’s fish and wildlife agency directly before applying for a license or participating. Use the state agency directory to open its official website.

I have an unresolved out-of-state citation. Who do I contact?

Contact the court or agency in the state that issued the citation. That issuing authority determines whether the citation has been satisfied and provides acceptable evidence of compliance. Keep copies of official court or agency documentation.

Can my home state decide whether I was guilty of the original charge?

The original case belongs in the issuing state. A home-state administrative review typically addresses whether the Compact-related action is properly applied under the home state’s law, not guilt or innocence on the original wildlife charge.

Can I appeal a Compact-related suspension?

Due-process and appeal procedures are governed by the law of the state taking the action. Read the notice you received and contact the named agency promptly; deadlines may apply.

Does the Compact cover fishing and marine species?

It can. The Compact definition of wildlife is broad and may include fish, mollusks, crustaceans, reptiles, amphibians, birds, and mammals. The controlling question is how the relevant state defines and regulates the species and privilege.

Does the Compact make all state wildlife laws the same?

No. States retain their own wildlife laws, licensing rules, suspension criteria, notice procedures, and appeal rights. The Compact provides communication, fair citation handling, and reciprocal recognition—not one national wildlife code.

Can I look up a person’s wildlife record on this website?

No. This website does not provide a public person search or expose protected personal records. If you need information about your own privileges, contact the relevant state wildlife agency.

Where should I report a suspected wildlife violation?

Use the wildlife crime reporting service, conservation officer contact, or law-enforcement channel for the state where the suspected violation occurred. This public site does not accept violation reports.

Using this website

Public information and privacy.

Why can’t this website tell me whether I am eligible?

Your eligibility depends on the law and records of the state where you want to participate. This site explains the Compact, but only that state’s wildlife agency can give you an answer about your own privileges.

Can I submit my citation or personal information here?

No. Do not send a citation, Social Security number, driver’s license number, password, or security code through a public website or ordinary email. Contact the court or agency named on your notice using its published contact information.

Protect your personal information

Use contact information printed on your citation or published by the state agency. This public site will never ask for your full Social Security number or payment information.
