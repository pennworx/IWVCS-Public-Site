A public overview

About the Compact.

The Interstate Wildlife Violator Compact helps participating states respond when wildlife citations and license suspensions cross state lines.

Why it exists

Wildlife privileges do not necessarily stop at a state border.

The Compact allows participating states to communicate about qualifying wildlife citations, convictions, failures to comply, and suspensions while each state continues to apply its own law.

Fair treatment when away from home

In qualifying cases, nonresidents may receive a citation without extra detention or bonding simply because they live in another state.

Consequences for ignoring a citation

A failure to comply with an out-of-state citation may be reported to your home state and may affect your privileges until the case is resolved.

Suspensions recognized elsewhere

Another participating state may recognize a suspension when the underlying violation would also support suspension under that state’s law.

History

From a western-state idea to nationwide cooperation.

Wildlife law-enforcement leaders began discussing an interstate approach in the early 1980s. Colorado and Nevada developed early proposals, and Colorado, Nevada, and Oregon enacted the founding legislation in 1989.

Participation expanded over the following decades. Current public guidance identifies 49 participating states, with Hawaii reported as being in the process of joining.

Interstate cooperation

The Compact supports cooperation across borders while every state retains its own wildlife laws and authority over license privileges.

Who decides what applies to you?

The relevant state does.

Your citation is handled where it was issued. Your home state applies its own law to any home-state action. A destination state decides whether you may obtain a license or participate there.

The Compact’s interstate leadership maintains the shared framework, but it does not replace state courts, wildlife agencies, hearing procedures, or appeal rights.

Use the right contact

Unresolved citation: contact the issuing court or agency.

Suspension or appeal: contact the state that sent the notice.

Travel eligibility: contact the wildlife agency in the state where you plan to go.

Need an answer about your privileges?

Contact the state where you plan to participate.

Only the relevant state can decide whether you may hunt, fish, trap, or obtain a license there.
