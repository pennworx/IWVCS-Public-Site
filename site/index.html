Start with your situation

What brought you here?

The Compact can matter when you receive a citation away from home, have wildlife privileges suspended, or plan to hunt, fish, or trap in another state.

You received an out-of-state citation

Follow the citation’s instructions and contact the issuing court or agency if anything is unclear. Ignoring it may affect your privileges at home.

What to do next

Your privileges are suspended

Do not assume the suspension ends at one state’s border. Other participating states may recognize it under their own laws.

Understand reciprocity

You are planning to travel

If you have any suspension, contact the destination state before buying a license or participating. Only that state can confirm your eligibility.

Where to get an answer

Start with the state that controls your privileges.

Only the appropriate state wildlife agency can confirm your eligibility, explain a suspension, or tell you what steps apply to your situation.

Nationwide cooperation

49 participating states, one shared framework.

The Compact began with Colorado, Nevada, and Oregon in 1989. Today, nearly every state participates. Hawaii is identified by a current state source as being in the process of joining.

Before crossing state lines

If your privileges are suspended in any state, contact the wildlife agency in every state where you intend to participate. Eligibility and appeal rights can differ by state.
