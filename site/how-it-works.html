What it means for you

What happens across state lines.

If you receive a wildlife citation away from home or have privileges suspended, the Compact may allow that information and suspension to follow you to other participating states.

The Compact matters to you in two main situations: you receive a wildlife citation outside your home state, or a state suspends your hunting, fishing, or trapping privileges.

Do not assume every state will treat you the same

Each state keeps its own wildlife laws and decides whether your underlying violation would support a suspension there. Notice, hearings, scope, and appeal rights can differ.

1. If you are cited away from home

In many qualifying cases, you can accept the citation and continue without posting a bond or being detained only because you live in another state. Read the citation carefully and respond exactly as directed.

This option does not apply in every case. State law, the seriousness of the offense, identity concerns, a required court appearance, a mandatory jail term, or a felony may require a different response.

2. If you are convicted or do not resolve the citation

You must respond according to the citation’s terms. If you are convicted or fail to comply, the state that issued the citation can send that information to your home state.

  • Failure to comply: the home state may begin a suspension action that remains in place until the issuing state provides satisfactory evidence of compliance.
  • Conviction: the home state records the conviction and considers it as though it occurred there for license-suspension purposes.

If the citation is unresolved, contact the court or agency named on it. Your home state generally cannot decide the original charge or mark the out-of-state case resolved.

3. Your home state may act

Your home state is the state of your primary residence. After receiving information from the state that cited you, it applies its own law and determines what happens to your wildlife license privileges.

You should receive the notice and review rights required by that state. A home-state review generally concerns whether the Compact action applies correctly; the original charge is handled in the state where you were cited.

4. Before you travel to another state

Other participating states may recognize your suspension when the underlying violation would also support suspension under their laws. Before buying a license or hunting, fishing, or trapping elsewhere, ask that state whether you are eligible.

The safest rule is simple: if any wildlife privilege is suspended, contact every state where you intend to hunt, fish, trap, or obtain a license before doing so.
Public guidance synthesized from participating state wildlife agencies

Activities the Compact can affect

“Wildlife” is defined broadly in the Compact and can include mammals, birds, fish, reptiles, amphibians, mollusks, and crustaceans when protected or regulated as wildlife under the relevant state’s law. A “license” can include a license, permit, or other public document authorizing a person to pursue, possess, or take wildlife.

Because states define and regulate wildlife differently, the same conduct or privilege may not receive the same treatment in every state.

What the Compact does not do

  • It is not a single national hunting, fishing, or trapping law.
  • It does not replace a state’s courts, hearing process, or appeal rules.
  • It does not provide a public search for another person’s record.
  • It is not a device for collecting unpaid installment balances after a court has already accepted a payment arrangement.
  • It does not replace a state’s responsibility to apply its own laws and procedures.

Terms you may see in a notice

Issuing state
The participating state that issues the wildlife citation.
Home state
The state of a person’s primary residence.
Compliance
Answering the citation through an appearance or an allowed payment of fines, costs, and surcharges.
Suspension
A revocation, denial, or withdrawal of some or all license privileges, including the ability to apply for or exercise a license.
Personal recognizance
An agreement, made when the citation is issued, that the person will comply with its terms.

Where to get an answer about your situation

For an unresolved citation, contact the court or agency named on the citation. For a suspension, appeal, or travel-eligibility question, contact the state wildlife agency responsible for the notice or the state where you plan to participate.
